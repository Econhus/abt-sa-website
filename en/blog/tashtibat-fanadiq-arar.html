Hotel Finishing & Fit-Out Contractor: A Practical Case Study from the O Grand Hotel Project

Hotel projects are among the most complex types of commercial contracting, because they combine structural and façade works, mechanical and electrical installations (HVAC, fire fighting, electrical), and precise interior finishing — all at the same time, where any delay or coordination error between these disciplines directly affects the hotel's opening date. With the growth in commercial hotel projects, the need for a general contractor able to manage this complexity within a clear schedule has become more important than ever.

In this article, we walk through the scope of work in hotel finishing projects, the most common mistakes that cost project owners time and money, and how the ABT Contracting (Aamar Al-Bayan Al-Thabit for Contracting) team executes this type of project — with real, documented photos from our ongoing project at the O Grand Hotel in Arar, which is still under execution.

Scope of Work in Hotel Finishing Projects

A typical hotel project goes through overlapping stages, not just a single phase:

  • Structural and façade works: the concrete structure, insulation and cladding of the external walls, and glass façades.
  • Mechanical works: HVAC duct networks and their insulation, and fire fighting systems (sprinklers) and their installation across all floors.
  • Electrical works: electrical wiring and supply compatible with the duct and fire system routing, without conflicts.
  • Interior finishing: suspended ceilings, painting, and flooring installation in rooms, corridors, and public facilities.

The critical point is that these works are not executed in strict sequence — they overlap. Ductwork, fire systems, and electrical work must be coordinated before the suspended ceilings are closed, otherwise any later change turns into costly reopening and reinstallation.

Common Mistakes That Cost Hotel Project Owners

  • Closing ceilings before inspecting the installations: installing the suspended ceiling or final paint before the HVAC ductwork and fire system have been inspected and approved later leads to reopening the ceiling and redoing the work at double the cost.
  • Multiple subcontractors with no single coordinating party: having each discipline (HVAC, electrical, finishing) with a separate contractor raises the risk of conflicts in locations and schedules, and makes it harder to identify who is responsible when something goes wrong.
  • Delayed preparation of finishing materials: a lack of early coordination between the supply schedule and the execution schedule stalls teams that are ready to work and delays final handover.
  • Overlooking local climate conditions when selecting materials: insufficient façade insulation or adhesives unsuited to the large temperature swings between summer and winter shows up on the building just months after handover.

What Determines the Price of Hotel Finishing Projects?

There is no fixed price for hotel projects, because every project differs in scope and complexity. The main factors that determine the price:

  • Required scope of work: whether it’s a full structure and finishing, finishing only on an existing structure, or mechanical and electrical works on their own.
  • Number of floors and building area: this directly affects the quantity of ductwork, fire systems, and electrical wiring required.
  • Level and type of finishing materials: there’s a big difference between economy materials and hotel-grade, high-durability materials built for heavy use.
  • Required schedule: compressing the execution timeline to speed up the opening may require larger parallel work crews, which is reflected in the cost.

That’s why we always base our proposals on a detailed bill of quantities (BOQ) that shows each item independently, instead of a lump sum that’s hard for the project owner to evaluate or compare against other offers.

Frequently Asked Questions About Hotel Finishing Projects

Does ABT Contracting handle full hotel contracting works, or finishing only?
The ABT team executes an integrated scope that includes structural and façade works, mechanical and electrical installations, and interior finishing and flooring under a single team, instead of splitting the project across multiple subcontractors.

How long does it take to execute a medium-sized hotel project?
The duration varies depending on the size of the hotel, the number of floors, and the required scope of work, which is why we set a detailed schedule after studying the drawings and a site visit instead of a rough general estimate.

Can finishing works start before the mechanical and electrical installations are fully complete?
Some finishing works can start in parallel in areas where the installations are complete and have been inspected, but it is always advisable not to close the final ceilings or walls before the HVAC, fire, and electrical systems in that specific area have been inspected and approved.

Do you provide a written warranty on hotel project works?
Yes, we provide a written post-handover warranty followed up by the same team that executed the work, whether for the mechanical and electrical installations or the finishing works.

Do you work on other commercial projects besides hotels, such as malls and hospitals?

How Do You Choose a Trustworthy General Contractor for Your Hotel Project?

  1. Ask to see real projects that are under execution or recently completed, with genuine photos rather than generic ones from the internet.
  2. Make sure the price quote is based on a detailed bill of quantities (BOQ) covering every discipline, not just a lump-sum figure.
  3. Ask about the coordination process between disciplines (HVAC, fire, electrical, finishing) before the ceilings and walls are closed.
  4. Check that there’s a written post-handover warranty, not just a verbal promise.
